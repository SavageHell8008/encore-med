# 13 — Medical Supplies Industry: Terminology & Taxonomy

**Scope:** Research into correct clinical/industry terminology and typical categorization patterns for consumable medical supplies — gloves, masks, adult diapers, catheters, catheter gel, urine bags, feeding tubes, suction tubes, PPE, and general consumables/accessories. Intended to ground EnconeMed's content and taxonomy in real terminology rather than invented or loosely-used consumer terms.

**Labeling key:** **[Official Requirement]**, **[Industry Best Practice]**, **[Recommendation]**, **[Observation]** — as defined in 01-business-understanding.md.

---

## Executive Summary

Consumable medical supplies terminology is well-documented by clinical sources (hospital/health-system patient education pages, standards bodies) and by real medical-supply retailers' category structures, which is more sourceable ground than the equipment-side "website structure" question in file 12. Standards exist and matter here in a way they don't for general merchandising: glove material grades are governed by specific ASTM standards (D6319 nitrile, D3578 latex, D5250 vinyl), and masks are governed by specific BIS standards in India (IS 9473 for N95-type respirators, IS 16289 for 3-ply surgical masks). Catheters have a clear, clinically-standard three-way split (indwelling/Foley, intermittent, external) that any content or taxonomy should follow rather than inventing alternative groupings.

Retailer-side categorization (how Vitality Medical, Shield HealthCare, 180 Medical, and similar sites organize these products) converges on groupings by clinical use-case (incontinence/urology, enteral feeding, wound/ostomy care) rather than by raw material or product type — an [Observation] worth adopting for EnconeMed's consumer-facing taxonomy, layered on top of, not replacing, correct clinical terminology in product copy.

## Important Findings

1. **[Industry Best Practice]** Glove materials fall into three clinically-recognized categories: nitrile (synthetic rubber, documented as the current de facto clinical standard for puncture resistance and hypoallergenic properties, governed by ASTM D6319 for medical exam gloves), latex (natural rubber, tactile-sensitivity use case, governed by ASTM D3578, carries allergy risk that has driven broad clinical shift toward nitrile), and vinyl/PVC (lowest cost, lowest barrier protection, governed by ASTM D5250, generally positioned for low-risk/short-duration use). AQL (Acceptable Quality Level) 1.5 or better is the cited benchmark for medical exam grade across these standards.

2. **[Official Requirement]** In India, N95-type respirator masks require BIS certification under IS 9473, and 3-ply surgical masks are covered under IS 16289. Government hospitals and medical-use sale of masks in India requires BIS certification — masks lacking it cannot legally be marketed/procured for medical use in that context. **[Recommendation]** Any mask product listed by EnconeMed as "N95" or "surgical grade" should have its BIS certification status tracked as a real, checkable product attribute, not asserted generically.

3. **[Official Requirement/Industry Best Practice]** Catheters have a standard three-way clinical classification: **indwelling/Foley catheters** (long-term, retained via inflatable balloon, connected to a drainage bag, typically changed monthly), **intermittent catheters** (single-channel, no retention balloon, inserted and removed several times daily — colloquially "in-and-out" catheters), and **external catheters** (non-invasive, external collection devices, primarily for men). Intermittent catheters further vary by length, size (French/Fr gauge), tip style (straight vs. coudé/curved tip), material, and coating (e.g., hydrophilic-coated vs. uncoated). **[Recommendation]** EnconeMed's catheter category content and product attributes should use this exact three-way split and the associated attribute vocabulary (French size, tip style, coating, balloon size for Foley) rather than inventing simplified alternative groupings.

4. **[Observation]** Real medical-supply retailers (Premier Ostomy, Health Products For You, Get Complete Care, and others referenced in search results) consistently group consumables by clinical use-case category rather than by raw material: "Incontinence and Urology" (catheters, diapers/briefs, leg bags, night drainage bags, irrigation supplies, accessories together), "Enteral/Feeding" (feeding tubes, pumps, formula, extension sets, backpacks), "Ostomy" (bags, skin barriers/wafers), and "Wound Care" (dressings, gauze, bandages) as adjacent-but-separate categories. **[Recommendation]** This use-case-first grouping (rather than a material-first or product-type-first grouping) is a reasonable, market-validated pattern for EnconeMed's consumer-facing taxonomy for supplies.

5. **[Observation]** "Catheter gel" (lubricant/anesthetic gel used with catheterization) and "urine bags" (leg bags for daytime mobile use vs. larger night drainage bags) are documented in retailer taxonomies as accessory/companion items nested under the catheter/urology category rather than as a standalone top-level category — supporting a "category + accessories" content model rather than flat listing of every SKU as its own top-level category.

6. **[Observation]** Feeding tubes and related enteral nutrition supplies (pumps, formula, gravity/backpack delivery kits) are consistently grouped as their own distinct category separate from urology/incontinence, and separate from general "wound care" — confirming these should not be merged into a single generic "consumables" bucket without use-case sub-grouping.

7. **[Official Requirement]** Because the 2025 LMPC amendment removed *medical devices* from standard packaged-commodity MRP/labeling jurisdiction (see file 01, Finding 2), and most listed consumables here (gloves, masks, diapers, catheters, urine bags) are generally treated as either "medical devices" under CDSCO classification (e.g., catheters, given invasiveness, are more likely device-classified) or ordinary packaged goods (e.g., basic diapers may not be device-classified) — the correct labeling regime is not uniform across this entire product family. **[Recommendation]** This must be confirmed per-SKU/category with a regulatory professional before publishing MRP-panel-style label content, rather than assuming one rule for "all supplies."

## Implementation Notes

- **[Recommendation]** Model the consumables taxonomy as: top-level use-case categories (Incontinence & Urology; Enteral/Feeding; Respiratory/PPE; Wound Care; General Consumables) each containing both primary products and their accessories (e.g., catheters + catheter gel + urine bags together under Incontinence & Urology), matching the observed retailer pattern in Finding 4–6.
- **[Recommendation]** Store material-type (nitrile/latex/vinyl for gloves; N95/surgical/cloth for masks) and clinical-type (Foley/intermittent/external for catheters) as structured product attributes, using the exact clinical/standards vocabulary from Findings 1–3, so that filtering and search can be built on correct terminology from the start.
- **[Recommendation]** Track BIS certification status (IS 9473 / IS 16289) as a real per-SKU attribute for any mask product, sourced from actual supplier documentation, not inferred from the product name.

## Common Mistakes

- **[Observation]** Using "N95" as a generic marketing term without confirming actual BIS IS 9473 certification of the specific product being listed — India-specific legal exposure given the cited requirement that BIS certification gates medical-use sale/procurement.
- **[Recommendation — anti-pattern]** Flattening catheters into a single undifferentiated "catheter" listing without the Foley/intermittent/external distinction — this is clinically meaningful (different use cases, different customers) and retailers in the research consistently preserve it.
- **[Observation]** Treating "vinyl gloves" as interchangeable with nitrile/latex in clinical-use marketing copy — sourced comparisons consistently note vinyl provides the least barrier protection and is positioned for low-risk/short-duration tasks only, not general clinical use.
- **[Recommendation — anti-pattern]** Applying one generic labeling/MRP template across all consumable categories without checking whether the specific product is CDSCO-device-classified (different labeling regime) or a standard packaged good (LMPC regime).

---

## Recommended Practices

- **[Recommendation]** Adopt the use-case-first taxonomy (Incontinence & Urology, Enteral/Feeding, Respiratory/PPE, Wound Care, General Consumables) as the customer-facing top-level structure for the supplies section, consistent with observed retailer convention (Finding 4).
- **[Industry Best Practice]** Use precise clinical terminology in product titles/descriptions (Foley vs. intermittent vs. external catheter; nitrile vs. latex vs. vinyl gloves; N95 vs. 3-ply surgical mask) rather than generic consumer phrasing, both for SEO/discoverability accuracy and to avoid clinically misleading copy.
- **[Recommendation]** Group accessories (catheter gel, urine bags/leg bags/night bags, extension sets, connectors) under their parent clinical category rather than as an undifferentiated "accessories" catch-all, per the observed retailer pattern.
- **[Recommendation]** Maintain a per-SKU compliance attribute (device-classified vs. standard packaged good; BIS-certified vs. not, where applicable) to support correct labeling content generation later.

---

## Things to Avoid

- Do not label any mask product "N95" in customer-facing content unless the specific SKU is verified BIS IS 9473 certified.
- Do not merge catheter types into one generic listing — preserve the Foley/intermittent/external distinction in both content and taxonomy.
- Do not assume a single LMPC-style MRP label template applies uniformly to every consumable — device-classified items (per CDSCO) and ordinary packaged goods (per LMPC) are on divergent labeling tracks post-2025.
- Do not present vinyl gloves as an equivalent substitute for nitrile/latex in clinical-use marketing without the accompanying barrier-protection caveat found in sourced comparisons.

## Future Considerations

- **[Recommendation]** As EnconeMed's supply catalog grows, periodically re-validate the use-case taxonomy against evolving retailer conventions (this research reflects a snapshot of a handful of sites, not an exhaustive survey).
- **[Observation]** BIS certification requirements and lists of certified mask manufacturers/products can change; any per-SKU BIS-status attribute should be treated as needing periodic re-verification, not a one-time entry.
- **[Recommendation]** Consider whether India-specific catheter sizing/terminology conventions (French gauge is international, but availability/brand naming may differ locally) need a further India-specific research pass before Phase 1 content is finalized — this document leaned on largely US-sourced clinical/retailer content (Cleveland Clinic, 180 Medical, Shield HealthCare, Vitality Medical) for catheter terminology specifics, which is clinically consistent but not India-market-verified for product availability/naming.
